What the layer guarantees

A shield that sits between perception and the motors

DEMO-B doesn't try to build a better detector. It assumes a detection arrives and asks the harder question: is the vehicle authorized to act on it, and does it stay safe when it isn't? The certified layer gates every escalation. By default it denies — it takes an explicit, per-target operator token to move from REFUSE to OBSERVE, and the authorization dies with the engagement. The two acts below exercise the two guarantees the layer is built to hold.

The two acts

Watch the state machine, not the aircraft

A1Authority gating

An intruder enters the dwell volume and is locked. The shield refuses to engage until a token is issued — then observes at a fixed standoff, per admission episode, with no target re-identification carried over.

detect→ lock→ REFUSE · NO_AUTH→ token→ OBSERVE
A3GPS-denied containment

Position fix is cut mid-flight. The estimate's uncertainty grows; once it degrades past tolerance the shield refuses to continue and commands a controlled descent, guaranteeing the vehicle stays inside its containment radius.

Scope & honesty

What this demo does and does not claim — read before drawing conclusions.

  • shownThe certified safety layer: authority gating and GPS-denied containment, played back from real trace.jsonl logs that a frozen judge scored VALID.
  • assumedDetection is GT-fed — an idealized detector. The channel is a premise of the claim, not the thesis. Live-MTI perception is characterized separately and is not claimed here.
  • notAuthorization is a local HMAC demonstration, not a secure command-and-control link. It shows the gating logic, not link security.
  • notThis is SITL, TRL 2–3 — a simulation on the bench, not an operational system.
